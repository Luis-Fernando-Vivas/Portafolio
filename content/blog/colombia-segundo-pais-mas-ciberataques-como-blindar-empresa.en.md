The latest report by Check Point Research revealed that Colombia ranks as the second most targeted country for cyberattacks in Latin America, surpassed only by Brazil. On average, every organization in the country faces more than 4,000 intrusion attempts per week. Far from being a headache limited to banks or multinationals, data from the Bogotá Chamber of Commerce (Cámara de Comercio de Bogotá) confirms that micro, small, and medium-sized businesses account for 70% of cybercrime victims. If you handle clients, orders, or data online, digital security is no longer an afterthought: it is a core pillar of your business survival.

## Why Attackers Target Small Businesses

There is a common myth that cybercriminals only go after large corporations. In reality, the opposite is true: large organizations invest millions in robust security perimeters, making small and medium-sized businesses much easier and more profitable targets.

Today, most attacks are neither manual nor personalized; they are run by **automated bots** that scan thousands of websites and digital infrastructures in seconds, searching for common vulnerabilities such as outdated plugins or misconfigured access controls. According to tech industry analyses from organizations like LinkTIC and ACIS (Colombian Association of Systems Engineers), the impact of ransomware or service downtime can cost a Colombian SMB between **470 and 520 million Colombian pesos** (around $110,000–$125,000 USD) in ransoms, business disruption, and brand damage. For many businesses, a blow like that means shutting down for good.

## Common Vulnerabilities in Your Digital Footprint

To safeguard your operations, the first step is knowing where attackers usually break in:

* **Unmaintained websites:** Platforms such as WordPress or online stores that go months without core, theme, or plugin updates. These missing updates leave security gaps that are widely known to attackers.
* **Unprotected contact and checkout forms:** Lead capture forms lacking anti-spam filters or security validations, leaving databases vulnerable to malicious code injection.
* **Weak and shared credentials:** Teams using the same simple password to access admin dashboards, hosting accounts, or business email, without requiring any multi-factor verification.
* **Lack of off-site backups:** Storing backups on the very same server hosting the website, meaning that if the server gets compromised, the backups are lost as well.

## Steps You Can Take Today to Protect Your Business

Securing your company does not require an enormous budget—just operational discipline and solid technical practices:

1. **Enable two-factor authentication (2FA):** Require a second authentication step across all critical logins: website admin dashboards, payment gateways, email accounts, and hosting panels.
2. **Schedule automated updates and regular audits:** Keep your CMS, themes, and plugins updated to the latest version. Remove any tools or plugins you no longer use.
3. **Automate cloud backups:** Set up regular, automated backups stored in a location completely independent of your primary hosting, so you can restore your site in minutes if anything goes wrong.
4. **Monitor logins and encrypt data:** Make sure you have active SSL certificates, an enabled web application firewall (WAF), and automated alerts configured to notify you of suspicious login attempts.
